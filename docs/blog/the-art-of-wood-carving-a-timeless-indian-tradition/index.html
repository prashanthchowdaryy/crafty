The Art of Wood Carving: A Timeless Indian Tradition

A short look at Indian wood carving and what to look for in a carved piece.

05 November 2026 · 2 min read

Wood carving has a long history in India. It decorates temple doors, palace windows, furniture and everyday objects, and the patterns, such as flowers, vines, geometric shapes and jali work, change from region to region.

Regional styles Different parts of India are known for different kinds of work. Saharanpur in Uttar Pradesh is known for carved sheesham furniture, Kashmir for walnut wood carving, and Karnataka for rosewood and sandalwood work. Inlay, where brass, metal or other materials are set into wood, is another old craft that is still practised today.

What to look for in a carved piece Look at the depth and the evenness of the pattern. The lines should be clean and the edges smooth to the touch. Check the joints, the legs and the underside, because good work is finished everywhere, not just on top.

Why small differences are fine Hand carving is never exactly the same twice. Small differences between two stools in a set show that they were made by hand, and they are part of the charm.
