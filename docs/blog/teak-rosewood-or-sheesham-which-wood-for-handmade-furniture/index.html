Teak, rosewood or sheesham: which wood for handmade furniture?

A plain guide to the three woods we use most, and what each one is best for.

28 October 2026 · 1 min read

When you order a handmade piece, the first question we ask is which wood you want. Each of the three we work with most has its own strengths.

Teak Teak holds natural oils that help it resist moisture and termites, so it lasts for generations. It starts golden and darkens slowly with age. It costs more, and it is the right choice for doors, mandirs and anything that will be used for decades.

Rosewood Rosewood is dense, dark and fine grained. It takes carving and polish very well, which is why it is used for chests and fine furniture. It is heavy and expensive, so it suits pieces meant to be heirlooms.

Sheesham Sheesham, sometimes called Indian rosewood, is more affordable and shows lively grain with a mix of light and dark tones. It works well for tables, trays and everyday furniture. Keep it away from constant damp.

How to choose Pick teak for durability, rosewood for richness and fine carving, and sheesham for value. Prices change with the size of the piece and the market, so ask for a quote. See our [handmade wooden pieces](/handmade-wood/) or [ask for a custom piece](/custom-orders/).
